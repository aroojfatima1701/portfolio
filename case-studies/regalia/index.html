A clinic website that starts with your face

The brief

Luxury that still feels safe

Before designing anything I went through clinic websites in Dubai. Most look alike: a stock hero, a long list of treatment names and a phone number. They give a visitor very little reason to trust them or to book.

People choosing an injectable or a laser want three things before they book: to know who will treat them, to know what it costs, and to know what the days afterwards will look like. Many also know their problem but not the name of the treatment that fixes it. So I set four goals. Lead with doctors and licensing. Show prices and downtime up front. Let people start from a concern on a face instead of a treatment menu. And give the owner one admin panel for both clinics, in English or Arabic.

Brand

The logo, kept exactly

The client arrived with a finished line-art logo: a crowned profile above a spaced serif wordmark. I used it exactly as supplied, only cutting it out cleanly and recolouring it to ivory for dark backgrounds. Everything else grew from it: the arch of the crown and head became the frame for every photo, and its plum ink became the main colour.

Photography

The treatment, doctor and face photos are free-licence images from Pexels, picked one by one for the clinic’s mood. They came from different photographers, so I colour-graded every one the same way: shadows pulled toward plum, warm highlights, softer saturation and clinical teal toned down. Side by side they read as one clinic.

Booking

A consultation in four steps

Clinic, doctor, date and time, details. A summary stays beside the form the whole way, and nothing is paid online.

  • Two cities, two clocksDubai shows Gulf time and closes on Fridays; New York shows Eastern time and closes on Sundays.
  • Prices per cityEach price is set in AED and USD separately in the admin panel, not converted, because the two markets are priced differently.
  • Consultation firstThe consultation fee is shown up front and is credited to any treatment booked afterwards.
  • Next free slotThe hero shows the next free consultation in the chosen city, worked out from live availability.
Arabic version

جمال يليق بكِ

Fully in Arabic, not just translated

In Dubai many patients prefer Arabic. One switch in the header turns the whole website into right-to-left Arabic: menus, all twelve treatment pages, the face map, doctors, quiz and booking.

  • Real Arabic typeAmiri for headlines and IBM Plex Sans Arabic for text. Letter-spacing and capitals are switched off, because they break joined Arabic letters.
  • Mirrored with careArrows, timelines, the city switch and drawers flip. Charts, phone numbers and the face map keep their natural direction.
  • Editable in bothThe owner can set an Arabic name and description for any treatment and Arabic hero text. Empty fields fall back to the built-in translation.
Mobile

Booked from the sofa

Most people browse clinics on their phone. The price card slides under the photo, the face map stacks above its results and the booking steps stay thumb-sized.

Admin panel

Runs both clinics

A custom panel for the clinic manager: appointments, treatments, doctors, memberships, clinic details and the homepage itself, with no monthly platform fees. It is fully interactive and designed to connect to a Laravel API.

It is wired to the website. A booking made on the site appears as a request to confirm, and a price changed here is live on the site straight away.

  • Doctors and membershipsAdd or edit doctors per clinic and change membership plans. The last doctor at a clinic can’t be deleted by accident.
  • Clinic hours drive bookingChanging opening hours or the consultation fee updates the slots and prices patients see.
  • Safe editsUnsaved changes show a save bar, deletions ask for confirmation, and uploaded photos are resized in the browser before saving.
Build

Built with React and TypeScript in a Next.js structure, with the data layer specified as a Laravel API.
